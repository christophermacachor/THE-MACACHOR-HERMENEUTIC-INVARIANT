## Abstract

This paper introduces the **Macachor First Derivative** as a proposed
theoretical framework for extracting structural information from an
underlying scalar substrate before that structure is decomposed into
conventional measurement, representation, and semantic categories.

The framework distinguishes three epistemic layers. First, established
mathematics provides constraints concerning differentiation,
reconstruction, information loss, and the dependence of integration on
anchoring conditions. Second, the Macachor formalism defines a scalar
substrate ψ and its first derivative ∇ψ as a conceptual architecture for
substrate-to-representation translation. Third, the framework advances
hypotheses concerning coherence, artificial intelligence, semantic
reconstruction, and quantum-state analogy that require independent
validation.

The central principle is:

> **No semantic claim should be treated as fundamental unless its
> structural provenance is identified.**

Within the Macachor formalism, the first derivative is the **minimal
structural translation layer**: it does not constitute meaning by
itself, but expresses how an underlying structure changes across a
domain. Higher derivatives may encode increasingly local or higher-order
behavior while losing information required to uniquely reconstruct the
originating structure unless appropriate boundary or anchoring
information is retained.

The paper therefore does not claim that ψ is an experimentally
established physical field of meaning. Rather, ψ is a defined
theoretical object. Its physical interpretation, if any, is an open
hypothesis.

## 1. Epistemic Hardening

A principal revision in Version 2.0 is the explicit separation of claim
types.

### E --- Established

Claims supported by established mathematics, physics, information
theory, or independently replicated empirical evidence.

### M --- Macachor Formalism

Definitions introduced by this framework. Examples include the scalar
substrate ψ, the first-derivative operator ∇ψ as a structural
translation layer, the coherence kernel, and the substrate-provenance
rule.

### H --- Hypothesis

Claims that extend the formalism into empirical or physical territory
and can, in principle, be tested, challenged, or falsified.

### A --- Analogy

Correspondences between mathematical structures and concepts in another
domain. An analogy is not treated as an identity.

This classification is mandatory throughout the framework.

## 3. Why the First Derivative Matters

**E --- Mathematical constraint:** differentiation can remove
information required for unique reconstruction of a generating function.
Reconstruction requires appropriate integration information and
anchoring conditions.

Therefore, higher-order derivatives alone need not uniquely specify the
original substrate.

**M --- Interpretation:** this motivates the Macachor distinction
between substrate, first derivative, and higher-order representation.

The resulting hierarchy is:

**substrate → first derivative → higher derivatives → representation →
measurement → semantics**

The first derivative is therefore called the **Macachor First
Derivative** or **structural ambassador**.

It is not claimed to be the only possible mathematical route to meaning.
It is the proposed minimal translation layer of this ontology.

## 4. Structural Provenance Rule

The governing rule of the hardened framework is:

> **Every semantic claim must identify the structural provenance from
> which its representation was derived, or explicitly declare that
> provenance unknown.**

This produces a provenance chain:

**SUBSTRATE**\
What underlying object is being described?

↓

**FIRST DERIVATIVE / COMPRESSION**\
What transformation exposes structural change?

↓

**COHERENCE**\
What preserves the relationship between representation and substrate?

↓

**VECTORIZATION**\
What direction, constraint, measurement, category, or perspective has
been imposed?

↓

**REPRESENTATION**\
How does the structure become numbers, symbols, tokens, equations,
language, or code?

↓

**SEMANTICS**\
What interpretation is assigned?

↓

**VERIFICATION**\
Can the interpretation be traced backward toward its structural source?

A result that cannot be traced backward is classified as
**interpretation**, not substrate-level knowledge.

## 5. Coherence

**M --- Definition:** coherence is the preservation of structural
correspondence between a representation and the substrate it purports to
represent.

This definition is deliberately broader than quantum coherence.

### Quantum coherence

**E --- Established:** physical quantum systems require control of
coherence and decoherence for quantum information processing.

### Macachor coherence

**M --- Framework concept:** structural coherence means that downstream
representation remains anchored to the generating structure.

The two concepts may be compared, but they must not be conflated.

## 7. Quantum Computing and the Qubit

The hardened argument is:

> **The qubit is an information-bearing representation implemented in a
> physical quantum system; the ability to manipulate that representation
> depends upon maintaining the relevant physical quantum state and its
> coherence.**

This does not imply that the qubit is unreal or secondary in practical
engineering. It means that the information abstraction should not be
confused with the complete physical substrate implementing it.

**M --- Macachor interpretation:** the deepest explanatory layer is
therefore sought in the coherent structural substrate rather than in the
symbolic labels assigned to states.

The stronger claim that all quantum fields constitute a single scalar
substrate remains a **hypothesis**, not an established result.

## 9. Artificial Intelligence

The framework proposes that an AI system can be understood as a powerful
representational compressor and assembler.

**H --- Structural-Anchoring Hypothesis:**

> If a generative system lacks sufficient access to the structural
> variables needed to uniquely constrain a target claim, then
> statistically plausible but structurally false reconstructions should
> occur.

This reframes hallucination as a problem of **underdetermined
reconstruction**, rather than asserting that hallucination is
mathematically inevitable for every current AI architecture.

The hypothesis generates testable questions:

-   Does explicit structural provenance reduce hallucination?
-   Does retrieval of primary-source constraints improve reconstruction?
-   Does bidirectional verification reduce semantic drift?
-   Can models distinguish substrate-derived claims from statistically
    inferred claims?
-   Can confidence be calibrated against provenance completeness?

prove it wrong.
